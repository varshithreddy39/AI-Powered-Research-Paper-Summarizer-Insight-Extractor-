Abstract: In this study, we investigate the neural mechanisms underlying perceptual decision-making tasks in ambiguous sensory environments. Using advanced neuroimaging techniques, we identified a novel role for prefrontal cortex fluctuations in modulating decision speed. Methods: Participants performed a two-alternative forced choice task while undergoing high-resolution fMRI. We measured both behavioral performance and neural activity across distributed cortical networks. Results: Analysis revealed that baseline prefrontal activity significantly predicted individual reaction times. Trial-by-trial fluctuations in this baseline correlated with decision confidence ratings. Conclusion: These findings suggest that decision-making processes are dynamically regulated by ongoing neural fluctuations rather than being fixed computations.
